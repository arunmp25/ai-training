Talking to AI

Before any jargon: what is it actually like to work with AI, and how do you get good results from it?

⏱ 1 min

🎤 Say No maths and no code. This topic is about what it is actually like to work with AI, and the habits that separate good results from frustrating ones.

  • Warm, simple opening: “No maths, no code. By the end of the course you’ll understand every buzzword you hear in AI meetings.”
  • Quick poll: “Who used an AI assistant this week? For what?” Use their answers as examples throughout.
Example

A familiar first attempt

Write an email to customers about the renewal.
Subject: An Important Update!
Dear Valued Customer, in today’s fast-paced world, insurance is more important than ever… (412 more words) …Warm regards, [Your Name], [Your Company]
Which customers? Which renewal? Who is [Your Name]? The model filled every gap with a confident guess. This topic is about closing those gaps.
⏱ 2 min

🎤 Say This is what most first attempts look like: a polite, generic email with a placeholder where your name should go. The AI did its best with what it was given, and it was given almost nothing.

  • Ask: “What would a person need to know to write this properly?” Collect answers: which customers, which product, what changed, tone, length. That list is the rest of the topic.
  • Open Behind the scenes to show how little the model was actually given.
The mindset

Think of AI as a brilliant new colleague who started today

Click each trait to see what it means for how you work with it.

⏱ 2 min

🎤 Say The most useful way to think about AI is as a brilliant colleague who started today. Very capable, but knows nothing about you, your company or your customers, and will guess rather than ask.

  • This framing does most of the work for the whole course. Every “trick” is just treating it like a smart new hire.
  • Emphasise “knows nothing about you” and “will guess rather than ask”. These two explain most bad AI results.
Where you meet it

Four ways AI shows up at work

Same kind of engine underneath, packaged differently. Click each one.

⏱ 1 min

🎤 Say You meet AI in four main places: chat assistants, features inside tools you already use, coding assistants and autonomous agents. Underneath it is the same kind of engine. What changes is the packaging around it.

  • Point out that it’s the same kind of model in all four. The packaging (what we’ll later call the harness) is what differs.
  • Ask which ones people already use.
Try it

Same request, three ways

You need to tell your team that Thursday’s project review has moved. Switch between the prompts and compare what comes back.

⏱ 2 min

🎤 Say Same request, three versions. The AI did not get smarter between them. We gave it what a colleague would need to do the job well.

  • Read the vague version first and ask: “Would you send this?” (No: wrong length, invented details, placeholders.)
  • Key point: the AI didn’t get smarter between versions. You gave it what it needed.
  • Topic 6 breaks a good prompt into its parts. This is just a taster.
Iterate

Don’t aim for perfect in one go. Have a conversation.

Step through a real refinement. Each short follow-up builds on everything said before.

💡 Small, specific follow-ups beat starting over. The AI sees the whole conversation so far (we’ll see how in Topic 5).
⏱ 1 min

🎤 Say Do not aim for the perfect prompt first time. Treat it as a conversation: ask, look, then refine with a short follow-up.

  • Stress that this is normal and expected. Experts iterate too.
  • Plant a seed: “How does it remember turn 1 at turn 4? Hold that thought for Topic 5.”

Habits

Six habits of people who get great results

Click each card to flip it.

⏱ 1 min

🎤 Say Six habits get most of the value: give context, be specific, show an example, iterate, check the output, and keep sensitive data out. Verifying is the one people skip.

  • These six habits are the practical takeaway for people who don’t go any deeper.
  • “Verify” is the one people skip. AI is fluent, and fluent isn’t the same as correct.

Takeaways

What to remember

🧑‍💼

Treat AI like a brilliant new colleague: smart, fast, and knowing nothing about you yet.

🎯

Be specific: audience, purpose, format, facts. Then iterate in short follow-ups.

🔍

Verify. It’s fluent, not always right. Live data and company facts need extra layers.
