The pipeline

Two phases: prepare once, look up on every question

Step through it.

⏱ 3 min

🎤 Say RAG has two phases: prepare the documents once in advance, then look things up on every question.

  • Indexing happens ahead of time (and again whenever documents change). Querying happens in a second, on every question.
  • The model itself never changes. We just give it better pages to read.

Step 1 matters most

Chunking: how you cut the document decides the answer

Question: “How many days can I work from home, and which days?” Change the chunk size and see what gets retrieved. Yellow = the sentences with the answer.

⏱ 3 min

🎤 Say How you cut the documents decides the answer. Chunks too small split the answer in half. Chunks too big add noise and cost.

  • Too small: the answer is split across chunks, so only half is retrieved and the answer is incomplete.
  • Too big: correct, but lots of irrelevant text, more cost and more distraction (Topic 4).
  • Just right: whole thoughts kept together. In practice teams also add overlap between chunks.
Chunking, properly

Chunk size and overlap: see the boundaries move

Real systems measure chunks in tokens (often 200–800) and let neighbouring chunks overlap so a thought cut at a boundary still appears whole in one chunk. Drag the sliders.

Overlap: words
⏱ 3 min

🎤 Say Overlap means neighbouring chunks share a few lines, so a sentence cut at a boundary still appears whole in one chunk.

  • It starts at size 16, overlap 0: the “Tuesday or Thursday” sentence is cut across two chunks. Drag overlap to ~8 and it’s whole again. Then try size 40: whole, but each chunk carries lots of unrelated text.
  • Strategies on the right: most teams start with structure-aware (split on headings/paragraphs) plus a token limit and ~10–20% overlap.
  • Every chunk keeps metadata: source, section, date, who may see it. That powers citations and permission filters (Topic 11).

Choosing

  
RAG, fine-tuning, or just paste it in?

  
For each situation, pick the best approach.

  

  
⏱ 3 min
🎤 Say RAG, fine-tuning or just paste it in? As a rule: RAG for knowledge, fine-tuning for behaviour, and paste it in for a one-off document that fits.
  • Rule of thumb: RAG for knowledge (facts that change and need citations), fine-tuning for behaviour (style, format at huge scale), and paste it in for one-off documents that fit in the window.

Takeaways

  
What to remember

  

    
📖 Open-book exam
RAG retrieves relevant chunks of your documents and adds them to the prompt. The model stays the same.

    
✂️ Chunking decides the answer
Whole thoughts per chunk, sensible size, a little overlap, metadata on every chunk.

    
🧾 Instruct the use
“Use only these sources, cite them, say when it isn’t there” turns retrieval into trustworthy answers.
