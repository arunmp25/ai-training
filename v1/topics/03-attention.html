Topic 03

Attention: how words look at each other

The single idea that made modern AI work, and the reason how you write a prompt changes what you get back.

⏱ 30 sec
  • Frame: “No maths. We’ll just watch it happen, then turn it into prompt-writing tips.”
A puzzle

What does “it” refer to?

You know instantly. The model works it out with attention: “it” looks back at every other word and decides which ones matter. Click any word, and switch between the two sentences.

⏱ 3 min
  • Ask the room first: “In sentence 1, what was tired? In sentence 2, what was wide?”
  • Change one word at the end and the meaning of “it” flips. Attention is how the model connects “it” to the right noun.
  • Click other words too, e.g. “tired” attends to “animal”.

Context changes meaning

“Bank”: river or money?

A word’s meaning is shaped by the words around it. Click “bank” in each sentence.

⏱ 2 min
  • This is why context is everything: the same word means different things depending on what surrounds it.
  • Link to prompting: if your prompt is ambiguous, the model resolves it using whatever other words are around, which may not be what you meant.
Many perspectives at once

Many “attention heads”, each looking for something different

The model runs many attention patterns in parallel: one tracks who, one tracks what, one tracks grammar… Switch heads.

⏱ 2 min
  • Analogy: a team reading the same sentence, each with a different highlighter colour.
  • Real models have many heads in many layers stacked up, which is how they handle long, complex instructions.

Why this matters for you · 2

Beginnings and ends get noticed most

Research found models tend to use information at the start and end of a long context best, and can miss things buried in the middle (“lost in the middle”). Move your key instruction.

Takeaways

What to remember

🔦 Attention
