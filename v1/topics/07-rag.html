Topic 07

RAG: an open-book exam for AI

Retrieval-Augmented Generation. Before answering, look up the relevant pages in your documents and put them on the desk.

⏱ 30 sec
  • One-line definition: RAG = search your documents, then add the results to the prompt, then generate the answer.
Why we need it

The model has never read your company’s documents

An employee asks the HR assistant a simple question. Compare the two.

⏱ 2 min
  • Without RAG it gives a confident generic answer: a hallucination about your policy.
  • With RAG it quotes the actual policy and cites it, so people can check.
The pipeline

Two phases: prepare once, look up on every question

Step through it.

⏱ 3 min
  • Indexing happens ahead of time (and again whenever documents change). Querying happens in a second, on every question.
  • The model itself never changes. We just give it better pages to read.
Step 1 matters most

Chunking: how you cut the document decides the answer

Question: “How many days can I work from home, and which days?” Change the chunk size and see what gets retrieved. Yellow = the sentences with the answer.

⏱ 3 min
  • Too small: the answer is split across chunks, so only half is retrieved and the answer is incomplete.
  • Too big: correct, but lots of irrelevant text, more cost and more distraction (Topic 03).
  • Just right: whole thoughts kept together. In practice teams also add overlap between chunks.
Searching by meaning

Embeddings: a map where similar meanings sit close together

Each chunk becomes a point on a “meaning map”. A question lands near the chunks that mean the same thing, even with no words in common. Pick a question.

🔎 Meaning search (embeddings): top 3
🔤 Old-style keyword search
⏱ 4 min
  • “Can I work from my sofa?” shares no words with “Employees may work from home”, so keyword search fails and meaning search nails it.
  • Real embeddings have hundreds of dimensions; this 2D map is a simplification.
  • The database that stores these points and finds the nearest ones is a vector database.
Choosing

RAG, fine-tuning, or just paste it in?

For each situation, pick the best approach.

⏱ 3 min
  • Rule of thumb: RAG for knowledge (facts that change and need citations), fine-tuning for behaviour (style, format at huge scale), and paste it in for one-off documents that fit in the window.
Failure modes

Where RAG goes wrong

When a RAG system gives a bad answer, it’s usually retrieval, not the model. Flip for the fix.

⏱ 2 min
  • Permission leaks are the scary one: the bot retrieves a salary document for someone who shouldn’t see it.

Takeaways

What to remember

📖 Open-book exam

RAG retrieves relevant chunks of your documents and adds them to the prompt. The model stays the same.

📍 Meaning, not keywords

Embeddings and vector databases find chunks that mean the same thing as the question.

✂️ Retrieval is everything

Good chunking, fresh data, permissions and citations make or break it.
